S1. By the definition of convergence, \(x_n \to a\) means that for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(N_a\) such that for all \(n\ge N_a\), \(d(x_n,a)<\varepsilon\).

S2. Similarly, \(x_n \to b\) means that for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(N_b\) such that for all \(n\ge N_b\), \(d(x_n,b)<\varepsilon\).

S3. Suppose, for contradiction, that \(a\ne b\). Since \(d\) is a metric, this implies \(d(a,b)>0\).

S4. Let \(\varepsilon=\frac{d(a,b)}{2}\). Then \(\varepsilon>0\).

S5. By S1 applied to this \(\varepsilon\), there exists \(N_a\) such that for all \(n\ge N_a\), \(d(x_n,a)<\varepsilon\).

S6. By S2 applied to the same \(\varepsilon\), there exists \(N_b\) such that for all \(n\ge N_b\), \(d(x_n,b)<\varepsilon\).

S7. Choose \(N=\max(N_a,N_b)\). For every \(n\ge N\), both \(n\ge N_a\) and \(n\ge N_b\), so
\[
d(x_n,a)<\varepsilon
\quad\text{and}\quad
d(x_n,b)<\varepsilon.
\]

S8. By the triangle inequality for the metric \(d\),
\[
d(a,b)\le d(a,x_n)+d(x_n,b).
\]

S9. Since \(d(a,x_n)=d(x_n,a)\), S7 and S8 give
\[
d(a,b)<\varepsilon+\varepsilon=2\varepsilon.
\]

S10. But \(\varepsilon=\frac{d(a,b)}{2}\), so \(2\varepsilon=d(a,b)\). Hence S9 yields
\[
d(a,b)<d(a,b),
\]
which is impossible.

S11. Therefore the assumption \(a\ne b\) is false, and so \(a=b\).
