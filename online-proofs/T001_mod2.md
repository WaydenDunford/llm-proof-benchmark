S1. Consider the linear system

$$
\dot x=Ax,\qquad x\in\mathbb R^n,
$$

and suppose there exists a symmetric positive-definite matrix \(P=P^T>0\) such that

$$
A^TP+PA<0.
$$

S2. Define the Lyapunov candidate

$$
V(x)=x^TPx.
$$

Because \(P\) is symmetric positive definite, \(V(x)>0\) for every \(x\neq0\), while \(V(0)=0\).

S3. Since \(P\) is real and symmetric positive definite, its eigenvalues are positive. Let

$$
m=\lambda_{\min}(P)>0,
\qquad
M=\lambda_{\max}(P)>0.
$$

The spectral bounds for quadratic forms give

$$
m\|x\|^2\le V(x)\le M\|x\|^2
$$

for every \(x\in\mathbb R^n\).

S4. Define

$$
Q=-(A^TP+PA).
$$

Since \(A^TP+PA\) is negative definite, \(Q\) is symmetric positive definite. Let

$$
q=\lambda_{\min}(Q)>0.
$$

Then

$$
x^TQx\ge q\|x\|^2
$$

for every \(x\in\mathbb R^n\).

S5. Along any solution \(x(t)\) of \(\dot x=Ax\), differentiation of \(V\) gives

$$
\dot V
=\dot x^TPx+x^TP\dot x.
$$

Using \(\dot x=Ax\),

$$
\dot V
=x^TA^TPx+x^TPAx
=x^T(A^TP+PA)x
=-x^TQx.
$$

S6. By the lower spectral bound for \(Q\),

$$
\dot V(t)=-x(t)^TQx(t)\le -q\|x(t)\|^2.
$$

By the upper bound \(V(x)\le M\|x\|^2\), we have

$$
\|x\|^2\ge \frac{V(x)}{M}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\dot V(t)\le -\frac{q}{M}V(t).
$$

S7. Set

$$
c=\frac{q}{M}>0.
$$

The scalar differential inequality

$$
\dot V(t)\le -cV(t)
$$

implies

$$
V(t)\le V(0)e^{-ct}
$$

for every \(t\ge0\).

S8. We first prove Lyapunov stability of the origin. Let \(\varepsilon>0\) be arbitrary and choose

$$
\delta=\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{m}{M}}>0.
$$

Suppose that

$$
\|x(0)\|<\delta.
$$

Then

$$
V(0)\le M\|x(0)\|^2
<M\delta^2
=m\varepsilon^2.
$$

S9. Since \(V(t)\le V(0)e^{-ct}\le V(0)\) for all \(t\ge0\),

$$
V(t)<m\varepsilon^2.
$$

Using the lower spectral bound \(m\|x(t)\|^2\le V(t)\), we obtain

$$
m\|x(t)\|^2<m\varepsilon^2,
$$

and hence

$$
\|x(t)\|<\varepsilon
$$

for every \(t\ge0\). Thus the origin is Lyapunov stable.

S10. We now prove convergence to the origin for every initial condition. Let \(x(0)=x_0\in\mathbb R^n\) be arbitrary. A linear system with constant matrix \(A\) has a solution defined for every \(t\ge0\), so the following estimate applies globally in time.

S11. Combining

$$
m\|x(t)\|^2\le V(t),
\qquad
V(t)\le V(0)e^{-ct},
\qquad
V(0)\le M\|x_0\|^2,
$$

gives

$$
m\|x(t)\|^2
\le
M\|x_0\|^2e^{-ct}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\|x(t)\|^2
\le
\frac{M}{m}\|x_0\|^2e^{-ct},
$$

and consequently

$$
\|x(t)\|
\le
\sqrt{\frac{M}{m}}\,
\|x_0\|e^{-ct/2}.
$$

S12. Since \(c>0\),

$$
e^{-ct/2}\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{as }t\to\infty.
$$

Hence, for every initial condition \(x_0\in\mathbb R^n\),

$$
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|x(t)\|=0,
$$

so

$$
\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)=0.
$$

S13. Thus the origin is Lyapunov stable and every solution, from every initial condition in \(\mathbb R^n\), converges to the origin as \(t\to\infty\). Therefore the origin is globally asymptotically stable.
